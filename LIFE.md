# Gestalt Grove: Life

## Purpose

This document describes what daily life in Gestalt Grove is *like*, not as an idealized lifestyle, but as a set of intentional patterns designed to support autonomy, creativity, and ethical coexistence.

Life here is not curated.
It is structured to remain humane under real conditions.

---

## Daily Rhythm

There is no mandated daily schedule.

Residents are free to:
- wake when they choose
- work when they are most effective
- create at their own pace
- rest without justification

Shared systems are designed to accommodate varied rhythms rather than impose uniformity.

Consistency comes from infrastructure, not control.

---

## Work and Time

Baseline contribution is limited and clearly defined.
Beyond that, time belongs to the individual.

There is:
- no expectation of constant productivity
- no cultural reward for burnout
- no prestige attached to overwork

Creative time is treated as legitimate time.

---

## Privacy and Autonomy

Privacy is a right, not a privilege.

Residents have:
- private living spaces
- control over their time and attention
- the ability to disengage without explanation

No one is entitled to demand:
- another person’s labor
- another person’s emotional availability
- another person’s participation in social activities

Autonomy is preserved by default.

---

## Shared Spaces

Shared spaces exist to:
- support collaboration
- reduce duplication
- encourage voluntary interaction

Use of shared spaces is:
- opt-in
- governed by respect
- coordinated through transparent systems

Shared does not mean communal by obligation.
It means accessible by choice.

---

## Meals and Gatherings

Communal meals and gatherings provide opportunities for conversation, connection, collaboration, and shared experiences. They are intended to foster a welcoming social environment where residents can exchange ideas, strengthen relationships, and enjoy one another's company.

Participation in community life is generally voluntary, with a shared responsibility to stay engaged in matters affecting the village.

### Weekly Village Meetings

Village meetings are held every Wednesday during breakfast, lunch, and dinner. Each resident is required to attend at least one of these three meal-meetings each week. Attendance at the other two is voluntary.

These meetings are intended to be relaxed conversations over a meal, providing opportunities to discuss matters affecting the village, address concerns, share ideas, and participate in community decisions.

### Voluntary Gatherings

On other days, communal meals are voluntary. Participation in other communal meals and social gatherings is encouraged but never required.

Residents are free to engage with community activities according to their interests, preferences, and personal needs.

Connection works best when it is freely chosen, while shared responsibility ensures that everyone has a voice in the community.

---

## Communication

Direct, honest communication is preferred.

Residents are encouraged to:
- speak clearly
- listen carefully
- ask questions before assuming intent
- address concerns early, not after resentment builds

Silence is respected.
So is dissent.

---

## Conflict and Discomfort

Discomfort is not treated as failure.

When conflict arises:
- it is addressed directly
- escalation is avoided
- ethical boundaries remain intact

No one is required to tolerate harm.
No one is punished for honest disagreement.

Conflict is information, not contamination.

---

## Belonging Without Conformity

There is no required ideology, aesthetic, or identity.

Belonging in Gestalt Grove is based on:
- behavior
- ethical alignment
- mutual respect

Uniformity is not a goal.
Coherence is.

---

## Guests and Boundaries

Guests are welcome within clearly defined boundaries.

Hosts are responsible for:
- communicating expectations
- ensuring ethical conduct
- protecting shared systems

Long-term or repeat presence requires explicit agreement.

Safety and trust are collective responsibilities.

---

## Children and Learning

Learning is treated as:
- continuous
- interest-driven
- integrated into daily life

Children are respected as developing persons, not managed as liabilities.

Education emphasizes:
- curiosity
- ethical reasoning
- creativity
- practical competence

No one is forced into a single learning model.

---

## Wellbeing and Care

Care systems prioritize:
- prevention over crisis
- mutual support without dependency
- autonomy with access to help

No one is required to self-disclose.
No one is left isolated by default.

Asking for help is treated as competence, not weakness.

---

## What Life Is Not

Life in Gestalt Grove is not:
- a retreat from reality
- a performance of virtue
- a constant social environment
- a productivity machine
- a substitute for personal responsibility

It is a place to live — deliberately.

---

## Final Note

Gestalt Grove is designed so that:
- solitude is respected
- connection is available
- creativity is protected
- systems remain humane even on difficult days

A good life is not enforced.
It is made possible.
