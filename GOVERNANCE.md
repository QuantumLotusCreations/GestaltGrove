# Gestalt Grove: Governance

## Purpose

Governance in Gestalt Grove exists to:
- enable coordinated action without coercion
- resolve disagreement without domination
- prevent power accumulation without paralysis
- ensure ethical consistency across all decisions

Governance is not a hierarchy.
It is a **set of processes** constrained by ethics.

No rule, vote, or decision is legitimate if it violates the Ethical Foundation.

---

## Foundational Principle

**All governance exists to protect and expand ethical creativity, not to exercise power.**

Authority is procedural, temporary, and revocable.
No person, role, or group governs by default.

---

## Decision-Making Model

### Consensus as a Design Constraint

Gestalt Grove uses **true consensus** for high-impact decisions.

Consensus does not mean:
- unanimity through pressure
- endless discussion
- emotional compliance

Consensus means:
- all participants can affirm the decision is ethical, fair, and workable
- unresolved objections are addressed, not overridden

If consensus cannot be reached, the decision is considered **incomplete**, not failed.

### Objections Must Be Generative

Any objection must include:
- a clear ethical or practical concern  
- at least one proposed alternative or modification  

Objections raised without constructive intent are treated as noise, not signal.

The burden of imagination is shared.

### No Majority Rule Override

Majority rule cannot override:
- ethics
- individual autonomy
- minority safety
- creative agency

Votes may be used to:
- prioritize options
- allocate limited resources
- measure preference

Ranked-choice and quadratic voting are candidate methods for different situations. Their precise applications and mechanics will be selected during implementation.

Votes may not be used to:
- coerce compliance
- justify harm
- silence dissent

### Preliminary Ballots and Decision Routing

Village decisions will begin with an online preliminary ballot through the village intranet, ensuring that every resident has an opportunity to participate. Each ballot will offer three choices: **Yes**, **No**, or **No Preference**.

The results of this initial ballot will determine how the proposal proceeds:
- **Unanimous `Yes`:** The proposal is approved.
- **Unanimous `No`:** The proposal is rejected.
- **Any `No` votes in a non-unanimous result:** The proposal moves into the consensus process, where concerns and objections will be addressed through discussion and revision.
- **A mixture of `Yes` and `No Preference`, with no `No` votes:** The proposal moves to an appropriate additional voting process.
- **Unanimous `No Preference`:** The proposal likewise moves to an appropriate additional voting process.

This allows the residents themselves to determine, on a case-by-case basis, which proposals require consensus and which can be resolved through another voting method.

## Scope-Based Governance

Decisions are made at the **lowest viable scope**.

- Individual decisions remain individual.
- Shared-space decisions involve only affected parties.
- Village-wide decisions are reserved for structural matters.

No decision should involve more people than necessary.

---

## Roles and Responsibilities

Functional Roles exist to coordinate and maintain the systems that support village life. They may involve ongoing responsibilities, specialized expertise, or work that cannot be reasonably divided into individual tasks.

Roles do not confer authority over other residents.

Specialized Roles are:
- voluntarily undertaken
- transparent
- time-bounded or reviewable
- revocable if misused

### Roles and Weekly Work Requirements

Residents serving in an ongoing Role fulfill their six-hour weekly community-work requirement through that Role. They are not required to complete an additional six hours of ordinary tasks.

A resident serving in a Role receives compensation equivalent to the full six-hour weekly baseline, even when the actual time required to fulfill the Role's responsibilities is less than six hours.

When a Role requires work exceeding six hours in a week, additional compensation must be approved on a case-by-case basis to prevent abuse while fairly recognizing necessary work.

These arrangements allow residents to concentrate on their specialized responsibilities while maintaining a predictable and equitable compensation structure.

### Rotational Task Inspections

Task inspections are a shared responsibility distributed among residents through a rotating schedule.

Residents are ordinarily expected to serve as inspectors when their turn arrives. However, residents whose ongoing Role responsibilities require their attention may be moved further along in the rotation so that they can remain focused on their primary work.

Time spent conducting task inspections counts toward the required six weekly hours and is compensated as part of the applicable work-payment system.

This approach distributes responsibility for verifying completed tasks without unnecessarily disrupting essential ongoing work.

### Weekly Village Meetings

All residents, including those serving in ongoing Roles, are required to attend at least one of the three Wednesday village meal-meetings each week.

Attendance at the other two Wednesday meetings and other voluntary communal gatherings remains optional.

---

## Conflict Resolution

Conflict is expected and treated as informational.

Resolution prioritizes:
1. direct conversation between involved parties
2. facilitated dialogue if needed
3. ethical containment if harm persists

Punishment is not the goal.
Protection of the system and its participants is.

---

## Containment of Unethical Behavior

When behavior violates the Ethical Foundation:
- access to systems may be limited
- roles may be revoked
- participation may be paused or ended

These actions are:
- preventative, not punitive
- reversible upon demonstrated change
- documented and transparent

Repeated unethical behavior indicates misalignment, not misunderstanding.

---

## Transparency and Accountability

Governance processes are:
- documented
- inspectable
- auditable by participants

No hidden rules.
No shadow authority.
No informal power structures tolerated.

Opacity is treated as a system failure.

---

## Adaptation Without Drift

Governance may evolve if changes:
- improve ethical clarity
- reduce friction without reducing agency
- increase resilience under stress

Governance may not evolve if changes:
- centralize power
- reduce accountability
- weaken ethical constraints

Stability comes from principles, not rigidity.

---

## Final Note

Gestalt Grove does not govern people.
It governs **processes**, so people remain free.

Good governance is quiet.
When it works, creativity flourishes and control becomes unnecessary.
