# Gestalt Grove: Ethical Foundation

## Purpose

Gestalt Grove exists to maximize creativity, autonomy, and sustainable flourishing without coercion, exploitation, or harm.

Ethics are not an overlay, guideline, or aspiration.
They are the **root system** from which all governance, economics, infrastructure, and culture grow.

Any system that violates its own ethics eventually collapses.
This document exists to prevent that failure mode by design.

---

## Core Ethical Principle

**An act is ethical if it increases creativity (or its logical equivalents) for at least one person, including the person acting, without diminishing creativity for anyone.**

The Bill of Ethics describes creativity as the product of ethical awareness and intelligence, expressed as `C = EI`.

It identifies two interconnected ways that creativity may be increased:
- **Through ethical awareness:** By increasing ethical awareness, love, personal evolution, growth, and their logical equivalents. These are described as mutually reinforcing resources whose increase necessarily increases the others.
- **Through intelligence:** By increasing intelligence that is used creatively rather than destructively. Access to objective truth, energy, and freedom are identified as enhancers of intelligence because they expand a person's ability to understand, predict, and influence their environment.

The Bill of Ethics recognizes that both ethical awareness and intelligence may have additional logical equivalents beyond those explicitly identified. Its definition of creativity encompasses both sets of resources while preserving the distinctions between them.

The Gestalt Grove Code of Honor further identifies love, awareness, evolution, and the availability of objectively true information to ethical people among creativity's logical equivalents. It also recognizes other resources, including freedom, privacy, autonomy, and energy, as creativity enhancers whose value depends on how they contribute to ethical creativity.

The guiding principle remains that an ethical act increases creativity, or its logical equivalents, for at least one person without diminishing creativity for anyone else, subject to the qualifications established in the Bill of Ethics.

## Non-Negotiable Commitments

The following principles are not subject to vote, compromise, or exception.

### 1. We do no harm.
Harm includes physical, psychological, emotional, intellectual, or systemic harm.

Self-defense against imminent unethical action is the sole exception.

---

### 2. We do good at every opportunity.
Inaction in the presence of preventable harm or unethical behavior is itself unethical.

Ethical systems require active maintenance.

---

### 3. Ethical ends require ethical means.
Unethical means always produce unethical outcomes.

Efficiency, urgency, popularity, or profit never justify ethical shortcuts.

---

### 4. No coercion.
No one may be compelled, economically, socially, or psychologically, to act against their ethical agency.

Power over others is never ethical except in immediate self-defense.

---

### 5. No exploitation.
No person’s labor, attention, creativity, or vulnerability may be extracted for another’s gain.

Contribution replaces rent.
Participation replaces obligation.

---

### 6. No delegation of unethical authority.
No individual may delegate authority they do not personally possess.
No group may perform acts that would be unethical if performed by an individual.

Majority rule does not override ethics.

---

## What This Enables

By enforcing these constraints, Gestalt Grove enables:
- high trust without naivety  
- creativity without precarity  
- cooperation without coercion  
- disagreement without domination  
- accountability without punishment  

Ethics are not a limitation on possibility.
They are what make durable possibility possible.

---

## What This Forbids

The ethical foundation explicitly forbids:
- domination disguised as leadership  
- manipulation disguised as consensus  
- exploitation disguised as opportunity  
- harm justified by ideology, urgency, or scale  
- tolerance of predatory or parasitic behavior  

Systems that require “special cases” to function are already broken.

---

## Learning, Doubt, and Correction

Certainty halts learning.
Doubt creates new questions.
New questions generate better systems.

It is ethical to:
- question assumptions
- revise designs
- accept corrective feedback
- admit error publicly

It is unethical to:
- suppress dissent
- avoid feedback
- defend broken systems out of pride
- mistake tradition for justification

---

## Enforcement Without Punishment

Ethical enforcement prioritizes:
- prevention over reaction
- limitation of harm over retribution
- removal of unethical leverage rather than punishment of persons

Those who repeatedly act unethically may lose access to systems, resources, or participation, not as punishment, but as containment.

---

## Living Document

This document is foundational but not static.

Revisions are permitted only if they:
- increase ethical clarity
- expand creative capacity
- reduce ambiguity without weakening constraints
- do not violate the Core Ethical Principle

Ethics evolve through refinement, not dilution.

---

## Final Note

Gestalt Grove is not designed for ideal people.
It is designed for **real people**, and for systems that remain ethical even when people are tired, wrong, afraid, or in disagreement.

Ethics are the operating system.
Everything else is an application.
