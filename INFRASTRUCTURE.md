# Gestalt Grove: Infrastructure

## Purpose

Infrastructure in Gestalt Grove exists to:
- meet essential needs reliably
- reduce dependency on fragile external systems
- remain maintainable by residents
- degrade gracefully under stress

Infrastructure is designed to be **unremarkable when working**, and understandable when it fails.

---

## Design Principles

All infrastructure systems adhere to the following principles:
- redundancy over optimization  
- simplicity over novelty  
- repairability over efficiency  
- local control over central dependency  
- transparency over abstraction  

No system is considered complete unless it can be explained, inspected, and maintained by the people who rely on it.

---

## Shelter and Structures

Gestalt Grove structures prioritize:
- durability
- energy efficiency
- disaster resistance
- long-term livability

The planned architecture centers on durable monolithic domes and tori. It includes a central Gathering Dhome, residential Dhomes, the Creatorsphere's art and engineering facilities, and public-facing Gateway Galleries.

Residential Dhomes will have varied layouts for individuals, couples, roommates, and families, including both self-contained and voluntarily shared configurations. Every residential Dhome will have its own attached climate-controlled aquaponics greenhouse. The number and arrangement of homes will depend on the land available; seventy-two residences represent an ideal target, not a fixed requirement.

Design emphasizes:
- thermally efficient forms
- passive heating and cooling
- modular construction
- repairable materials
- layouts appropriate to each household

Shelter is treated as essential infrastructure, not a speculative asset.

---

## Water Systems

Water infrastructure is designed around conservation, reuse, and resilience.

This includes:
- rainwater capture and storage
- filtration suitable for potable and non-potable use
- greywater separation and reuse
- gravity-fed systems where possible

No single water source is treated as indispensable.

### Passive Water-Harvesting Earthworks

Site preparation will incorporate passive, hybrid water-harvesting earthworks, including back-sloping terraces, hügelkultur trenches, and hügel-swales, to capture runoff, retain moisture, and support the outdoor multi-layered food forest.

The approach is documented in greater detail in [Site Preparation — Passive Water-Harvesting Earthworks](site-planning/WaterHarvestingEarthworks.md), including the original 15-acre reference scenario and its adaptation to other potential sites.

## Food Systems

Food production prioritizes:
- year-round availability
- low external input
- ecological integration
- nutritional sufficiency

Approaches may include:
- aquaponics
- food forests
- soil-based cultivation
- seed saving and preservation

Food systems are designed to feed residents first.
Surplus distribution is secondary.

---

## Energy Systems

Energy infrastructure emphasizes:
- renewable generation
- distributed sources
- storage and load balancing
- manual fallback options

Primary goals:
- meet essential needs
- avoid single points of failure
- minimize external dependence

Energy systems are chosen for reliability and maintainability, not novelty.

---

## Sanitation and Waste

Sanitation systems prioritize:
- water conservation
- pathogen safety
- material recovery
- minimal environmental impact

Waste systems are designed to:
- reduce volume
- separate streams
- enable reuse or safe disposal

Waste is treated as a design problem, not a moral failure.

---

## Connectivity and Communication

Connectivity supports:
- coordination
- education
- creative exchange
- emergency communication

Systems may include:
- external internet access
- local networks
- offline-first tools
- mesh or peer-to-peer communication

Connectivity is a tool, not an obligation.

---

## Maintenance and Repair

All infrastructure requires maintenance.

Design assumptions:
- maintenance is normal
- failure is expected
- repair knowledge is shared

Critical systems are documented.
Repair skills are distributed.
No system depends on a single expert.

---

## Scaling and Limits

Infrastructure scales deliberately.

Expansion occurs only when:
- existing systems are stable
- maintenance capacity exists
- governance remains effective

Growth is constrained by care, not ambition.

---

## Failure Modes (Explicitly Considered)

Infrastructure design explicitly accounts for:
- component failure
- supply interruption
- human error
- weather extremes
- gradual degradation

Systems are designed to fail locally rather than catastrophically.

---

## Evolution Without Fragility

Infrastructure may evolve if changes:
- improve resilience
- reduce maintenance burden
- increase ethical alignment
- do not introduce new dependencies

Upgrades are reversible where possible.

---

## Final Note

Infrastructure in Gestalt Grove is not a showcase.

It is a quiet, reliable foundation that allows people to:
- eat
- drink
- rest
- create
- repair

When infrastructure demands attention, it has already failed.
