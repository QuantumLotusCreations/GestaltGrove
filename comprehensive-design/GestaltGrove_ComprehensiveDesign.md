# Building a Sustainable Today

Permaculture Meets Sustainable Living,
Arts, and Engineering in Gestalt Grove.

Hello. My name is Neḷum-Rasa (Neḷu for short).  
  
Fourteen years ago, I released this idea (under a different name) as it existed in my imagination at that time, mostly to see what sort of response it would receive. Since then, with the aid of bold and inquisitive artists and engineers in search of a home, experience, and technological developments – it has evolved.  
  
And though I’m just as eager as many, if not more so, to live in this village, I’m grateful that I’ve had this much time to freely sort out the details. It feels far more complete than when it was first proposed, and it will continue to evolve.  
  
So, think of this village, not as a school or commune or farm, but as another operating system for a way of life. Sure, I’m not the first to have an idea of this nature – and I certainly won’t be the last – but I’m comfortable in saying that this one definitely has some unique characteristics.

Some highlights:

Growing food all year with climate controlled aquaponics greenhouses (meaning we’ll grow both food and fish without the use of fertilizer or chemicals).

Also growing food by way of food forest and hügelkultur walls.

Disaster-resistant, energy-efficient dome-shaped structures (can’t be blown away, washed away, or burned. Can even survive earthquakes to an extent - can’t do much about it if the ground opens beneath it).

The use of natural energy (solar, zero-point, etc), rainwater collection, greywater plumbing & filtration, and other sustainable technologies for basic utilities.

Residential Dhomes will use dome and torus-based designs in a range of layouts for individuals, couples, roommates, and families. Every residential Dhome will include its own attached climate-controlled aquaponics greenhouse. Homes will incorporate private living and bathroom facilities appropriate to their layouts, including incinerating toilets, solar-powered tankless water heaters, and foot- or gravity-pump sinks and showers as described in the residential systems plan. Residents will not pay conventional rent; instead, they will complete six compensated hours of village-supporting tasks each week. Residents also receive a separate weekly Circuitree stipend by virtue of residency.

Gestalt Grove will support every type of art and engineering possible.

Experiential school with interest-driven education that nurtures creativity, critical thinking, and sustainable living from an early age.

Gestalt Grove isn’t about creating utopia - we’re crafting a conscious, sustainable way of life.

!!Introduction

The following Bill of Ethics was written by [[Robert Podolsky|http://www.titanians.org/bob-podolsky]] and Gregory Sulliger in 1993. It is an interpretation and extension of the work of [[John David Garcia|http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_David_Garcia]], as presented so thoroughly and clearly in his book, //''Creative Transformation''//. 

Organizations of all types can use it to amend or define their founding constitutions or by-laws.

!!Preamble

We, the undersigned residents constituting a quorum of Gestalt Grove hereby adopt the following //''"Bill of Ethics"''// as the highest priority policy for governing all our future actions and procedures, both in our dealings with those outside our organization and in our relationships with members and/or employees within our ranks. Henceforth all other written and unwritten rules of conduct for persons associated with this organization shall be understood, reinterpreted, or if need be revised to conform to the definitions and principles stated in this Bill of Ethics.

!ARTICLE 1: Philosophy & Rationale of This Bill of Ethics

1.1 WHEREAS this organization exists for the pursuit of ''ethical'' purposes by ''ethical'' means;

1.2 WHEREAS the charter of this organization establishes the right of its resident-artists to alter and reform governing policies as they may think proper; and

1.3 WHEREAS the resident-artists/members of this organization have expressed their belief that the establishment of a Bill of Ethics would substantially promote the rights and well-being of all who come in contact with this organization;

1.4 THEREFORE the policy of this organization is hereby amended... this Bill of Ethics being appended thereto.

!ARTICLE 2: Definitions

2.1 We believe it to be self evident that people are neither "good" nor "evil" except as their acts are "good" or "evil"

2.2 And that a person’s actions are "good" (or equivalently "just" or "ethical") if they increase the creativity of at least one person, including the person acting, without limiting or diminishing the creativity of any person, including the person acting.

2.3 Since creativity is the product of ethical awareness and intelligence (as symbolized by the equation: C = EI), there are two ways an act may increase creativity:

2.3.1 An act may increase creativity by increasing someone’s ethical awareness, degree of personal evolution, love, and/or growth... these creativity enhancers being logical equivalents of one another, in that any act which increases one of them must necessarily increase the others, and vice-versa;

2.3.2 An act may increase creativity by increasing the intelligence of any person who uses their intelligence creatively rather than destructively; where access to objective truth, access to energy, and freedom are enhancers of intelligence, since they increase one’s ability to predict and control the environment or to initiate and maintain causal relationships between events in the observable world.

2.4 The lists of equivalent creativity enhancers given above are incomplete. There may in fact be an unlimited number of such equivalences that apply. Hereinafter we shall use the words, "ethical awareness" to include all of its logical equivalents, and the word "intelligence" to similarly encompass all of its logical equivalents. The word "creativity" will be used to encompass both of the preceding sets of resources... the distinctions between the two sets being duly noted.

2.5 From the preceding it logically follows that it is ethical to limit or reduce a person’s intelligence in order to stop or prevent that person from acting destructively (unethically). This is generally accomplished ethically by limiting or reducing that individual’s access to intelligence enhancers.

2.6 Whereby "person" is meant any being having awareness of its own awareness… thus excluding forms of life whose actions are merely "natural"; and to whom this Bill of Ethics does not apply.

2.7 And acts which limit or reduce another person’s creativity (or any of the equivalent resources listed in Section 2.2 above) are – with the exception explained in Section 2.5 above – generally "bad", or equivalently "evil", "unethical", or "entropic"

2.8 And further, that good and evil acts by aware beings fall on an ethical continuum… where the best (most ethical) acts are those which contribute the most to the evolution of an individual or a group… and the worst (most unethical) acts are those which most increase the entropy (chaos or disorder) thereof;

2.9 And further still, that acts which are not "ethical" according to Section 2.2 above and which are not "unethical" according to Section 2.7 above may be said to be "ethically neutral", "innocent", "trivial", or merely "natural".

!ARTICLE 3: Principles

3.01 From the foregoing self-evident truths we infer that to act ethically each person must do their utmost to maximize creativity and its equivalents;

3.02 That ethical actions always increase someone’s creativity;

3.03 And that ethical actions never destroy, limit, or diminish anyone’s creativity except as described in Section 2.5 above.

3.04 And from the foregoing we infer that ''unethical'' means can never achieve ethical ends… this principle rejecting the notion that we can ethically sacrifice the creativity of the individual for the "greater good" of society, the "many", and so forth; from which it follows that:

3.05 ''Unethical'' means always produce unethical results (ends); trivial means always produce trivial results at best; and similarly...

3.06 ''Means which are not ethical ends in themselves are never ethical'';

3.07 From the foregoing it is also apparent that ''inaction is unethical''. Creativity cannot be passively expanded or increased… this must be done actively to overcome entropic destruction inherent in the Second Law of Thermodynamics. This principle is basically equivalent to the adage that, //"For evil to triumph, it is only necessary for good people to do nothing."//

3.08 It also follows that ''it is unethical to tolerate unethical behavior''. To do so is to violate Section 3.07 above. For this reason we are ethically bound to defend ourselves and others actively against injury or deceit when we or they are imminently imperiled by another’s unethical behavior; from which:

:3.08.1 It follows that it is unethical to augment (enhance) the creativity of anyone whom one reasonably believes will use such augmented resources unethically… and it is therefore ethical to withhold the augmentation of creative resources from anyone whose ethical commitment one reasonably distrusts; and furthermore:

3.09 It is ethical to learn and unethical to be certain. When one closes one's mind on a subject, they cease to learn… to increase their own awareness and creativity. Learning always increases creativity; and

3.10 It is ethical to doubt. By ceasing to have doubts about a subject, one becomes certain about it... and thus has ceased learning. Doubts create new questions …some of which yield new answers. Doubt is one of the cornerstones of creativity.

!ARTICLE 4: Laws, Rules And Regulations Compatible With This Bill of Ethics

4.1 Be it understood that the proper role of an organization's laws, rules and regulations is to empower those people acting singly or in concert who would embrace the foregoing //''Definitions''// and //''Principles''// set forth in Articles 2 and 3 above and who are willing to make the moral commitment to live their lives as ethically as they can… as suggested by Section 3.01.

4.2 And it is also the proper role of laws, rules and regulations to prohibit, by the most ethical means possible, any actions which are unethical as defined above.

4.3 Nor is it ever the proper role of rules and regulations to intrude, coerce, or interfere, in the lives of any people except as is truly necessary in order to accomplish the aims of Sections 4.1 and 4.2 above …such intrusion, even then, to be that which is minimally required.

4.4 Moreover, whenever the laws, rules, and regulations of an organization are in conflict with said //Definitions// and //Principles//, the ethics shall prevail …the rules being deemed to exist solely as the servant of the ETHICS, the latter being always superior to the rules.

4.5 Responsibility for Actions: Under the aegis of ethical rules and regulations compatible with this Bill of Ethics:

:4.5.1 All people are responsible for their own actions and the consequences which result from those actions. In determining who shall bear the burden of financial or other costs when someone’s actions result in harm to another person, ultimate (though not sole or total) responsibility rests with the individual who had the last available opportunity to prevent such undesirable effects from occurring.

:4.5.2 Also, responsibility under ethical rules is not mitigated by the failure of an individual to understand, comprehend, rationalize, or anticipate the consequences of his or her acts... except as such failure may alter the availability of opportunities to prevent harm from occurring.

:4.5.3 In any case, persons who enact harm on others in a self-induced state of mental incompetence (e.g. intoxicated) may still be required to bear the costs of the consequences of their actions... when the act of inducing such incompetence was, chronologically, the last opportunity anyone had to prevent the unethical act from being performed.

:4.5.4 Harm enacted by one person on another is solely justifiable when necessary in self or another’s defense against the person harmed.

!ARTICLE 5: Cooperation of Officials

5.1 Non-Interference: No elected or appointed official, officer, or employee shall take direct or indirect action or exert direct or indirect influence which would result in the circumvention, deflection, abrogation, evasion of or interference with the purpose of this Bill of Ethics.

5.2 Penalty: Any person found to be violating Section 5.1 above shall be reprimanded or removed from their position office or appointment as determined by the authority cited below.

5.3 Jurisdiction: Jurisdiction for purposes of this Bill of Ethics, shall be with the board of directors of this organization.

!ARTICLE 6: Previously Existing Rules and Policies

6.1 Conformity: Henceforth all the rules, regulations, and policies of this organization, whether they originate at board, executive, managerial, supervisory level or below shall be brought into compliance with this Bill of Ethics within 28 days of this date.

6.2 Wherever this amendment conflicts with or contradicts other rules, regulations, or policies, be they written or unwritten, this measure shall supersede and take precedence over the other, this one being the ultimate touchstone for valid procedural regulation throughout this organization.

## Gestalt Grove Ethic

''An act is ethical if it increases creativity (or any of its logical equivalents) for at least one person, including the person acting, without limiting or diminishing creativity for anyone.''

1. To act ethically, each person must strive to maximize creativity and its logical equivalents.

2. Ethical actions always increase someone’s creativity without destroying, limiting, or diminishing anyone’s creativity.

3. Unethical means can never achieve ethical ends and always have unethical consequences.

4. Means which are not ethical ends in themselves are never ethical.

5. It is unethical to tolerate unethical, predatory, or parasitic behavior and therefore inaction in the presence of these behaviors is unethical.

6. It is ethical to learn and unethical to be certain; and therefore it is ethical to doubt.

7. The exercise of power over others is never ethical except in the case of self defense against the imminent aggression of others.

8. The taking of another’s property by theft, fraud, deceit, or coercion of any kind is never ethical.

9. No individual can legitimately delegate to another, nor to a group of any kind, authority that the individual does not themself possess.

10. All laws, rules, and regulations are ethical and legitimate only insofar as they forbid and/or intervene in the commission of acts that are unethical, predatory, or parasitic. Government edicts that violate this principle are not truly laws at all.

> It is worth noting that all prosperity and happiness can be causally traced back to someone’s creativity, or one or more of its logical equivalents.

# Community Structure & Governance

### Communal Meals

On Wednesdays, breakfast, lunch, and dinner will be communal. Attendance will only be required for one of these meals as this is when we will hold village meetings.  
  
For every other day, only dinner will be communal and attendance will not be required. The reason attendance will not be required for the majority of the communal meals is because, while it would be nice if everyone attended every meal, we don’t want any one to feel forced or obligated.  
  
With the aid of digital technology, each artist that plans to attend a communal meal can select what they wish to have from an online menu and collect their meal upon arrival (sort of like placing an online pizza order for carry-out). Each order will be prepared and packaged individually (and only when placed), this eliminates the often unsanitary buffet-style layouts or the limited choices caused by mass produced dishes.

### Weekly Meetings

Meetings will be held every Wednesday during breakfast, lunch, and dinner. Attendance will only be required for one of the meals, though it will likely be helpful if some people attend all three (and possibly more productive if everyone attends all three).  
  
A primary feature of weekly village meetings is that they will be more "conversations over a meal" than "official community meetings". The idea is to establish a more relaxed atmosphere, with no set structure apart from the productive necessity of one speaker at a time. Regardless of how long it takes, whatever needs addressing, will be addressed.

## Decision-making

Village decisions will begin with an online preliminary ballot through the village intranet, ensuring that every resident has an opportunity to participate. Each ballot will offer three choices: **Yes**, **No**, or **No Preference**.

The results of this initial ballot will determine how the proposal proceeds:
- **Unanimous `Yes`:** The proposal is approved.
- **Unanimous `No`:** The proposal is rejected.
- **Any `No` votes in a non-unanimous result:** The proposal moves into the consensus process, where concerns and objections will be addressed through discussion and revision.
- **A mixture of `Yes` and `No Preference`, with no `No` votes:** The proposal moves to an appropriate additional voting process.
- **Unanimous `No Preference`:** The proposal likewise moves to an appropriate additional voting process.

This allows the residents themselves to determine, on a case-by-case basis, which proposals require consensus and which can be resolved through another voting method.

When a proposal enters the consensus process, it will be brought to the next village meeting for discussion. Concerns will be explored, alternatives considered, and adaptations made until everyone involved can agree upon an ethical and fair resolution. Even a single `No` vote is sufficient to initiate this process when the initial ballot is not unanimously `No`.

Given enough time, patience, and imagination… a reasonable compromise can be reached in any situation… one that all involved can agree is fair.

Residents expressing objections will be expected to explain their concerns and participate constructively in developing potential solutions. Everyone will be encouraged to speak openly and honestly, regardless of how they feel about how they feel (i.e., "this might be a dumb question, but…"). This will make things far more efficient, ensure that everyone is truly satisfied with the results of the discussion, and allow disagreements to become opportunities for understanding and improvement.

For proposals that proceed beyond the initial ballot without requiring consensus, Gestalt Grove intends to explore decentralized decision-making methods such as Ranked-Choice Voting and Quadratic Voting. Different methods may be appropriate for different circumstances, and their specific applications will be determined when needed.

Holacracy is also among the approaches being considered for organizing functional roles and responsibilities within the village.

All decision-making processes remain subject to Gestalt Grove's ethical foundation.

### Blockchain-Based Governance

To ensure transparency and fairness, voting will be conducted via blockchain-based governance systems using smart contracts, allowing residents to have an equal say in the direction of the village and how resources are allocated. This will ensure every vote is publicly verifiable while maintaining voter anonymity.

### Voting Methods for Routine Decisions

Routine matters may use ranked-choice voting, quadratic voting, or other appropriate methods, depending on the decision. These are candidate approaches for later implementation, not a single finalized voting procedure. Their use must remain consistent with the ethical foundation, and decisions requiring genuine consensus remain subject to that requirement.

### Crypto-Token Voting

Governance may use a separate token-based voting system to support secure participation and auditable results. The precise ballot and token mechanisms, including how they would implement ranked-choice or quadratic voting, will be chosen for each applicable use case when needed. Governance tokens are distinct from Circuitree, the village currency. Circuitree may be used to reward civic engagement where appropriate.

The following things will not be tolerated, and will be grounds for exile from the village:" violence; hunting; Vindictive Sabotage (tampering with any of -but not limited to- the following, especially with destructive or harmful intent: art; food; water; technology; structures; plant life; animal life); plagiarism; possession, production, or distribution of dangerous illegal drugs.  
  
Repeated violation of the following rules can also result in exile:" bullying; sneaking in unapproved guests; physical property theft, repeated neglect of weekly required work hours.

- Working as an inspector of completed tasks when it’s your turn. See "Tasks and Task-Inspections" for more details

### Guest Policy

For the safety of all living in the village, first-time guests must be approved in advance of their arrival (this is only necessary for the first visit). Last minute guests will be approved on a case by case basis.  
  
If the guest will be a regular guest, they will be required to keep a guest badge so they are not mistaken for a rogue tourist and asked to leave. (The badge will feature useful information… such as a map, transportation times, show times, and various other things to do or see in the the village.)  
  
Guests are not to be in private areas (areas that require keys to access) without a resident escort.  
  
Guests must abide by the same rules as villagers.  
  
Long-term guests, those that stay overnight on a regular basis, must be approved as such themselves (a similar process to becoming a resident) and contribute to the village as all residents do. (Note: this option will not be available until after the first residents are settled in.)

## Tasks, Jobs, & Wage-setting

### Wage-setting

Wages will be determined collectively using token-based voting on pay ranges for various categories of work.

- **Wage Categories and Ranges:** We will determine various categories into which tasks are likely to fit. Residents will then independently submit what they would accept as a range of pay for each task category, then use an appropriate collective decision-making method to establish the pay ranges. The precise voting method will be selected when the wage-setting process is implemented. The voted wages will become the pay ranges for their respective categories.
- When determining wages, the highest amount will be limited to the decided maximum amount one can gain from a particular task.
- **Dynamic Wage Adjustment:** Every six months, a wage review will occur to reassess the pay ranges. Villagers will vote on necessary adjustments, keeping wages responsive to inflation and changes in task difficulty.
- **Token-Based Voting on Wage Adjustments:** Residents will propose and vote on wage adjustments for each job category, ensuring fairness and consensus.

### Jobs

The only difference between tasks and jobs is whether or not you’ve completed your six hours for the week. Anything in addition to the required six hours is considered a "job" and paid as such.  
  
So while each villager will only be required to complete six hours of work in the village per week, everyone will be welcome to do more, and will be compensated for it too. However, instead of being paid by the hour, villagers will be paid per job… and each job will have its own appropriate rate (which will be set by the villagers themselves – see wage-setting for more details).

### Permanent Positions

There will be endeavors that can’t be fairly paid as "jobs" or "tasks" because they’re the types of endeavors that "take as long as it takes" or, require some level of specialized knowledge. It may also be more beneficial to have only a certain number of people handling certain things (maintenance and repairs on complex machinery, for example).  
  
In some cases, the permanent position will be the resident’s only required position (not counting inspections, that’s required for every resident). Whether or not this is the case will be decided upon during wage negotiations for the specific position.

### Tasks

Each resident will be required to contribute six hours, per week, to "tasks" that aid in the continued operation of the village. These tasks could be anything from emptying the trash bins in the public and purpose buildings to being a model for a sculptor or painter. (Note: artistic and engineering tasks may require an audition, as per the posting-creator’s discretion.)  
  
You can do all six hours in one day, or disburse them throughout the week in whatever fractions work best. Partial pay will be provided for completed hours if valid reasons are given for not completing all six hours. Neglecting to complete all six required hours (for no good reason) will result in that many more hours being required the following week, and may even result in exile in extreme cases (i.e., working for zero hours on a regular basis without good reason).  
  
Residents will be paid 120 Circuitree (the village’s currency) per hour upon satisfactory completion of all 6 hours (each additional task completed to meet the required 6 hours, adds an additional 24 Circuitree to the original hourly amount). Satisfactory completion will be determined by task inspections (see below). Neglecting to complete all six hours during the week (without good reason) will also result in no pay for those tasks until all six hours are complete.  
  
Tasks will be sorted into various priorities based on time-sensitivity. So, for example, harvesting food will hold a fairly high -if not the highest- priority. Cleaning of public spaces and kitchens will also be priority. Further categories will be established as a group when necessary. Feel free to share your ideas.

### Task Selection

Residents will choose tasks via the intranet, ensuring equal access to available work and efficient task management.

- **Task Matching Algorithm:** To streamline the process, a task-matching algorithm will suggest tasks based on a resident’s skills, availability, and interests, maximizing efficiency and ensuring the right people handle the right tasks.
- **Task Prioritization & Notifications:** Urgent or time-sensitive tasks will be automatically prioritized, with notifications sent to residents. This ensures important tasks are completed in a timely manner.
- **Task Progression:** Once a task is selected, it will be moved to a "pending completion" list. Upon completion, it will be then be moved to a "pending inspection" list.
    - When a task is completed and moved to the "pending inspection" list, the inspectors for that week will be alerted and presented with the ability to coordinate with one another.
    - An organizational (notification, communication, coordination) system will be set in place specifically for inspectors, for the sake of efficiency and sanity.

The task-management system will follow the village's governance principles. Specific coordination or voting mechanisms will be chosen as needed for their intended purpose.

### Task Inspections

Vital tasks, if not all tasks, will receive inspection after completion. Inspection will be conducted by other villagers, in groups of three, immediately after a task is completed. Two to three groups of inspectors will be active at a time, Detailed checklists will be established for consistent evaluations, and villagers can appeal inspection results through a formal appeal process, which will be reviewed by a neutral group.

- **Choosing inspectors:** Villagers will be listed in chronological order, from oldest villagers (those who have lived in the village the longest) to newest. The first three on the list will be the first inspectors. Every week, the next three on the list will be chosen. When we reach the end of the list, we start again from the top. Provided the population of the village isn’t divisible by three, this should also result in some variation between the groups of three.

Acting as an inspector also counts towards required weekly hours.

# Circuitree

Gestalt Grove will establish its own cryptocurrency, called Circuitree, designed to support the unique needs of our village. Circuitree will serve as the backbone of our community’s economy, encouraging fair distribution of resources, active participation, and collective growth.

> Circuitree and Gestalt Grove's governance system are separate but interoperating systems. Circuitree is the village currency and economic blockchain; governance uses its own voting tokens and governance mechanisms. Smart contracts may connect the two where appropriate (i.e., rewarding participation in governance with Circuitree) but Circuitree itself is not the voting token or governance system.

### Purpose & Vision

Circuitree is designed to be a cryptocurrency accessible to all residents and visitors, built on its own custom blockchain with smart contract capabilities. This will enable decentralized, secure transactions for everything from weekly stipends to the purchase of village goods and services. The currency will also support automated processes like task payments, project funding, and relevant integrations with the village's separate blockchain-based governance system.  
  
Circuitree will evolve based on the village’s collective input and priorities. Its _Proof of Elapsed Time (PoET)_ consensus mechanism ensures that contributions to village life—rather than wealth or computational power—are what sustain and validate the network.

### Stipends

Each resident will receive a weekly Circuitree stipend by virtue of residency, separate from any compensation earned for completing work. The stipend can be used for purchasing essentials (such as compostable toilet paper) from the village’s supply dome. It is not contingent on completing weekly work hours or passing task inspections; those are conditions governing task compensation, not the residency stipend. The stipend will be 450 Circuitree per week for the resident’s first twelve weeks and 720 Circuitree per week thereafter.

### Inventory

Inventory management will be made more efficient by using Circuitree for internal transactions. For example, when residents collect supplies from the dome, they will use their Circuitree to make purchases. This process will automatically track what has been taken and what needs restocking, minimizing the need for external supply trips and allowing us to secure wholesale prices for necessary goods we cannot produce within the village.

### How Circuitree is Created

1. **Proof of Frequency (PoF)**
   - **Functionality:** Weekly auto-creation and distribution of native tokens to a predefined list of addresses.
   - **Distribution Rules:**
     - **New Addresses:** Receive 450 tokens for the first 12 weeks.
     - **Existing Addresses:** Receive 720 tokens from week 13 onward until removal.
   - **Additional Allocation:** The same total amount distributed to all eligible addresses is also created and sent to Gestalt Grove's main address.

2. **Proof of Participation (PoP)**
   - **Functionality:** Creates and distributes native tokens upon receiving specific signals (e.g., task completions).
   - **Distribution Rules:**
     - Tokens are sent to the participant's address and Gestalt Grove's main address based on predefined signals.
     - **Triggering Event:** The signal for task compensation is sent after task completion has passed the applicable inspection; job payments follow their own completion conditions.
   - **Additional Allocation:** The same amount distributed to the participant's address is also created and sent to Gestalt Grove's main address.

3. **Proof of Elapsed Time (PoET)**
   - **Functionality:** Assigns validation opportunities to residents based on their long-term engagement and participation.
   - **Key Attributes:**
     - **Time-Based Contribution:** Rewards consistency and long-term involvement.
     - **Village Participation:** Increases validation eligibility based on participation metrics.
     - **Equality of Opportunity:** Randomized selection ensures fairness regardless of wealth or access to technology.

### Proof of Elapsed Time (PoET)

**Time-Based Contribution**:  
Every resident is randomly assigned opportunities to validate based on how long they’ve been active (involved in village life, contributing to tasks, etc.). This rewards consistency rather than intense but short-lived effort.  
  
**Village Participation**:  
Validation eligibility could increase the more integrated a resident is into the village’s ecosystem—this could be measured by their participation in tasks, attendance at meetings, or engagement in events. This encourages steady involvement over time.  
  
**Equality of Opportunity**:  
Since the selection of validators would be randomized based on participation time, everyone—regardless of wealth or access to high-powered technology—has a fair chance to contribute to the blockchain.  
  
**Non-Technical Contributions**:  
Instead of requiring proof of hardware or wealth, validation opportunities could come from non-technical contributions, such as managing community projects, creating art, or teaching others skills. The goal is to recognize that all types of contributions help maintain the community.

### Smart Contracts

With Circuitree, smart contracts will enable:
- Automatic distribution of weekly residency stipends.
- Automatic distribution of compensation for tasks and jobs upon their applicable completion and inspection conditions.
- Artist and engineer tokens, triggering payments upon project completion or public viewings of creative works.

### Stability

We are committed to fostering a stable, flourishing economy within the village. Circuitree will maintain internal stability through features like:
- Demurrage Fee: A small monthly fee applied to Circuitree holdings, encouraging circulation of the currency and discouraging hoarding. Residents with moderate savings for long-term projects are minimally impacted, while larger, stagnant balances are incentivized to flow back into the economy.
- Stabilization Fund: A reserve fund created through a percentage of Circuitree transactions, dedicated to supporting the currency during market fluctuations or economic downturns.

### External Exchange

In the early stages of the village, Circuitree will focus on internal uses to prevent speculation and maintain economic balance. However, as the village grows, residents will have the option to:
- Exchange Circuitree for widely accepted cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin or Ethereum, or even for fiat currencies (like USD) to meet external obligations.
- Use Circuitree in partnerships with external businesses that accept our currency.

A portion of village profits will be dedicated to maintaining an exchange fund, allowing residents to convert Circuitree to US Dollars at a 1:1 rate for a small fee, ensuring easy access to external financial opportunities.

### A Community-Driven Currency

Circuitree is built around the idea of collective ownership, economic sustainability, and empowering residents. It reflects our village values, promoting balance, creativity, and shared prosperity. Regular community forums and governance meetings will allow every resident to have a voice in shaping Circuitree’s future, ensuring it evolves to serve the best interests of all.

### Privacy

Built-in privacy features (like zero-knowledge proofs) will ensure that private transactions, such as inventory management or non-public income, are protected.

# Food

## Food Production

### Aquaponics Systems

We’ll utilize varied and elaborate aquaponics setups housed in numerous climate-controlled greenhouses, allowing us to grow vegetables, fruits, and herbs using nutrient-rich water sourced from fish tanks. The plants filter the water and return it to the fish, creating a closed-loop system. These systems maximize space, reduce water usage, and provide year-round food production.  
  
Multi-tiered vertical aquaponics systems will be housed in greenhouses, taking advantage of vertical space while maintaining optimal growing conditions year-round. The fish will not only provide food but also the necessary nutrients for plant growth. This symbiotic relationship between fish and plants offers a highly efficient means of producing food in a relatively small footprint.  
  
Each residence will have its own private greenhouse. The three dormitori in the Creatorsphere will share two greenhouses. And the village will share three greenhouses in the Creatorsphere, four greenhouses attached to the gathering dhome, and one large greenhouse near the Aquaponics Café.

### Hügelkultur

The village will adopt hügelkultur as a key method for outdoor gardening. Hügelkultur mounds are raised beds constructed from layers of wood, organic material, and soil. These mounds retain moisture, reduce the need for external irrigation, and provide long-lasting nutrients as the wood decomposes. This method also allows for vertical growing space.  
  
**Hügelkultur Walls:** To combine functionality with security, we plan to build 1.5-meter-tall hügelkultur walls. These walls will surround key areas of the village, including the residential zones, the entire village perimeter, and the crop field/festival campgrounds. The walls will provide not only a physical barrier but also additional gardening space, supporting a variety of plants while helping to maintain privacy and security.

### Companion Planted Food Forests

Our food forests will replicate natural ecosystems, planting trees, shrubs, herbs, and vegetables in layers. These systems support biodiversity and promote natural pest control while enriching the soil. We’ll use **companion planting** principles to ensure that plants grow in harmony, providing mutual benefits like improved soil health and reduced need for artificial fertilizers.

### Indoor Hügelkultur

In our pursuit of year-round food production, we are exploring the possibility of smaller indoor hügelkultur beds within controlled environments. These beds would harness the same decomposition and nutrient cycling principles while being adapted to indoor conditions.

# Infrastructure & Technology

## Water

Our village will use a combination of sustainable water collection, conservation, and filtration methods to ensure a reliable supply of clean water while minimizing environmental impact. Below is an outline of our water infrastructure:

### 01
#### Dew Collection Systems
The village will employ innovative dew collection systems. These systems capture atmospheric moisture, particularly during humid nights or early mornings, to provide an additional water source, especially useful during dry seasons or in arid environments. The setup will involve condensation surfaces strategically placed to maximize water capture.

### 02
#### Solar-Powered Tankless Water Heaters
Hot water will be provided through solar-powered tankless water heaters. These heaters use the sun’s energy to provide instant hot water without the need for large, constantly heated tanks. Solar panels will power these systems, making them both efficient and eco-friendly.

### 03
#### Gravity-Powered Sinks and Showers
Wherever possible, gravity-fed systems will be used. These systems store water in elevated tanks, allowing gravity to provide the pressure needed for sinks and showers without any need for pumps or electricity.

### 04
#### Greywater Recycling
Separate pipelines will be installed to capture and direct greywater for treatment.

- Greywater Plumbing: Separate pipelines will be installed to capture and direct greywater for treatment.
- Greywater Filtration and Sanitation: This system will include sedimentation, biofilters, and basic chemical treatments to purify the greywater for reuse in irrigation, and potentially in hügelkultur systems.

However, greywater will not be used directly in aquaponics due to the sensitivity of these systems. It will only be applied in areas where safety and soil health can be maintained, such as in hügelkultur or food forests. The filtration system will ensure water quality is suitable for these uses.

### 05
#### Foot-Pump Sinks and Showers
To conserve water and encourage more conscious use, foot-pump sinks and foot-pump showers will be installed throughout the village. These systems will:
- Use foot-operated pumps to draw water, ensuring that only the necessary amount is dispensed.
- Be especially beneficial in public and outdoor areas where water usage needs to be monitored.

For showers, a pressurized tank system will provide comfort while maintaining efficiency. Water will be pumped into a pressurized tank, giving residents a steady flow of water without relying on electricity or large pumps.

### 06
#### Rainwater Collection and Filtration
Rainwater will be one of our primary water sources. We’ll implement an advanced rainwater harvesting system, consisting of:
- Galvanized Steel Water Tanks: These durable, corrosion-resistant tanks will store rainwater for use in the village. Sizes will vary depending on the needs of each area, such as residential, agricultural, and public spaces.
- Advanced Filtration System: To ensure the rainwater is safe for drinking and other uses, we’ll use a multi-stage filtration process:
    - Sediment Filter: Captures large particles like dirt and debris.
    - Carbon Filter: Removes odors, chlorine, and organic compounds, improving taste and overall water quality.
    - UV Sterilization: Disinfects the water, eliminating bacteria, viruses, and other pathogens.
    - Reverse Osmosis: A final layer of filtration for removing heavy metals, chemicals, and any remaining impurities.

This comprehensive filtration ensures we have access to safe, potable water for everyday use.

### 07
#### Ground Wells and Live Water
Due to concerns about pollution and varying water table levels, we won’t rely on ground wells or live water sources. While they can be useful in certain regions, we are prioritizing rainwater collection and other above-ground systems to avoid contamination and water shortages. This approach also aligns with our environmental goals by focusing on renewable and clean water resources.

## Internet Access

### 01
#### Solar-Powered Communication Hubs
Recent innovations in solar-powered devices have allowed the creation of solar-powered hubs that maintain network connectivity in off-grid environments. These hubs can support satellite or mesh connections, ensuring the village stays online even in power outages.

### 02
#### 5G Fixed Wireless Access (FWA)
Recent advancements in 5G have made Fixed Wireless Access a reliable option for connecting rural or off-grid areas. If there’s a 5G tower within reach, a 5G receiver can be installed at the village, providing a fast internet connection without the need for traditional wiring. This offers a promising alternative for communities located near urban peripheries.

### 03
#### Outernet (Rebranded as Othernet)
Outernet, now **Othernet**, offers a satellite-based data delivery system where users can download web content like news, weather, and educational materials. Although it doesn’t provide full internet access, it’s a valuable source of information for off-grid communities, and its availability has improved over the years.

### 04
#### Maidsafe / Safe Network
Maidsafe, also known as the Safe Network, continues to focus on decentralized data storage and browsing. Though it was first proposed in 2014, it’s still in development, aiming to offer a decentralized internet without servers. This is suitable for secure file sharing and communications within your community. However, it’s not yet widely adopted and may be more experimental at this stage.

### 05
#### LoRa (Long Range Radio) Networks
LoRa is a low-power, wide-area network (LPWAN) technology that can send small amounts of data over long distances. It’s useful for sensors and basic communication, especially in off-grid settings. With a LoRa network, devices within a large radius (up to 10 km) can communicate, making it suitable for villages with dispersed residents.

### 06
#### Mesh Networks (Meshnets)
Meshnets are decentralized networks where each device connects directly to nearby devices, creating a resilient web. This allows for communication without the need for traditional internet providers. Popular systems include:

- CJDNS: Secure mesh routing protocol that creates encrypted tunnels between nodes.
- Althea: A mesh network system that incentivizes people to share bandwidth in exchange for cryptocurrency.

Meshnets can be ideal for community-based networks, offering redundancy and decentralization. They’ve seen significant development since 2012, with improved scalability and security.

### 07
#### Low-Earth Orbit Satellite Internet
Since 2012, satellite internet has advanced significantly. Services like Starlink and OneWeb provide low-latency internet access using satellites in low Earth orbit (LEO). These systems are now far more accessible, offering coverage in remote areas, making them a good option for off-grid living.

- Starlink: A rapidly growing satellite network by SpaceX, providing high-speed internet globally.
- OneWeb: Similar to Starlink, focusing on connecting underserved regions with LEO satellites.

While satellite internet is reliable, it’s worth having mesh networks as a backup to avoid reliance on any single infrastructure.

## Sanitation

The village’s sanitation system will be a blend of advanced technologies and eco-friendly practices, ensuring sustainability, minimal waste, and hygiene.

### 01
#### Incinerating Toilets
We will install Incinolet incinerating toilets, which burn waste into sterile ash without water. These toilets use electric heat to incinerate waste at high temperatures, reducing it to clean, odorless ash that can be disposed of easily. Incinolet units are highly efficient, environmentally friendly, and perfect for remote locations where plumbing infrastructure is not feasible. They save water, eliminate the need for sewage systems, and are low maintenance.
- _**Why Incinolet?:**_
    - Incinolet toilets offer an effective and clean alternative to traditional sanitation. Their ability to operate without plumbing or a continuous water supply aligns with our village’s sustainability goals. The ash byproduct is minimal and can be safely disposed of or used as a soil amendment in non-agricultural areas.

### 02
#### Rotating Composters
In addition to incinerating toilets, the village will use rotating composters for organic waste management. These enclosed systems will break down food scraps, garden waste, and other biodegradable materials, converting them into nutrient-rich compost. The rotating mechanism ensures even decomposition and reduces the need for manual turning, speeding up the composting process.
- _**Why Rotating Composters?:**_
    - These systems are ideal for maintaining clean, odorless composting and producing high-quality compost for use in village gardens and landscaping. They also minimize methane emissions by keeping compost aerated, helping reduce our environmental impact.

### 03
#### Greywater Systems
For wastewater from sinks, showers, and washing machines, greywater systems will filter and recycle water for non-potable uses, such as irrigating the village’s gardens. This system will help reduce water waste and maximize resource efficiency.

### 04
#### Composting Dome
The composting dome will serve as a weather-protected structure to house multiple rotating composters, ensuring safe, efficient composting year-round. With adequate ventilation, the dome will manage large-scale organic waste and composting needs, keeping the composters shielded from severe weather while maintaining optimal conditions for decomposition. This setup will be essential for converting organic material into nutrient-rich compost to support the village’s gardens and other agricultural systems.

# Community Life & Activities

## Meals

### The Gathering Dome Kitchen

The Gathering Dome will have an all-hours kitchen, from which all meals are free (but limited to three per person per day). The Gathering Dome is also where communal meals and meetings will be held.

### The Aquaponics Café

The Aquaponics Café, a combination of aquaponics greenhouse and café with courtyard seating… will be open to residents and tourists for breakfast and lunch.

### The Lounge Restaurant

The Lounge Restaurant, in the recreational dome will serve as an evening restaurant to residents and visitors.

The option to order a delivered meal will be available for each of these kitchens. Simply log on to the respective kitchen/café/restaurant’s page and select an item from the menu.

### Food Truck

Additionally, a daytime food vendor will traverse the village regularly in a neighborhood electric vehicle modified to serve as a food truck. The idea here is variety, so the vendor will carry everything from cold deserts, to natural snacks, to hot meals. Operating the truck, like with anything else in the village, counts towards the required weekly work hours. Any additional hours worked will be paid the wage established, by the residents, for this job.

## Social Activities

Fun stuff!

### Games

The village will have occasional game days with prizes and such. Participation won’t be mandatory, but observation could be entertaining. Games we might play: OMEGAball (will explain later), kickball, bubbleball (will explain later)… video games, board games, card games… something else that will be decided upon as a group.

### Celestial Events

For just about every celestial event (definitely every night-time celestial event), the village will have some form of a get-together. Grilled foods, camp-fires for cool nights, maybe a bit of music and dancing… will pair nicely with a good meteor shower.

### Seasonal Celebrations

Being as the village will be a bit of a nature sanctuary, equinoxes and solstices will be celebrated. Exactly how each one will be celebrated will be established by the residents as a group… so we may choose to adopt some rather old celebratory practices… or perhaps we’ll create entirely new ones that feel fitting for the seasons.

### Harvest Parties

Some, maybe most… possibly all… large harvests will need to be done by as large a group as possible. The larger the group, the lighter the work. After the harvest, we’ll have a nice party with dinner and performances and such.

### Social Clubs

Someone once inquired as to if the village could have an asexual social club. The reply, "Sure. Why not?"  
  
We recognize that humans like to do these things, so if a group of residents decide to form a common-interest-based social group that meets and does things, that’s perfectly fine. We only ask that the groups be inclusive… that is to say, if someone wants to join the asexual social club, but isn’t actually asexual, they should still be allowed to do so because they’re obviously interested.

### Holidays

Annually agreed upon holidays from any faith or tradition… (Saturnalia, Holi, The Day Out of Time)

And other activities not yet imagined!

## Shared with the public

Our sanctuary will strive to provide artists and engineers with access to diverse creative and technical resources. In addition to sharing food, we will offer in-depth knowledge on our sustainable growing practices and the innovative technologies that support our community.

### Foods

Once we’ve reached a point where we produce more food than we can consume and store, we will launch various programs for distributing food to those who may not otherwise have access to fresh food.  
  
One such program will involve physically delivering food on a regular bases to those who request it through a form on the public website (or maybe even by phone or letter).  
  
Another involves allocating a certain amount of each harvest to free distribution through the website, where the person placing the order is only required to pay for the shipping (they’ll also be limited to how much of each item they can order).  
  
We may also work with other venues like food banks and farmers’ markets.

### Tours

Village tours… accessibly priced, discounted in Bitcoin, even cheaper in Circuitree.  
  
The current idea is that a small tour group will be guided through the village on a 10-passenger golf car… to the museum, the theatres, the purpose torus to see some of the artists and engineers at work (if they agreed to be part of the tour), a meal at the café, maybe even a relaxing ride through the food forest… The extent and specifics of these tours will be decided upon by the villagers at a time when it is necessary.

### Live Performances

Live performances will likely be a regular occurrence once the village is fully operational.  
  
Performances open to the public will almost always take place in the public theatres of the Gateway Galleries or on the festival grounds of the Crop Field/Festival Campgrounds.  
  
Village-only performances will have the option of performing pretty much anywhere in the village that can accommodate the performance.

### Live Demonstrations

Live demonstrations of engineering projects will likely be a regular occurrence once the village is fully operational.  
  
Public demonstrations will primarily take place in designated innovation spaces in the Creatorsphere, or on the open demonstration grounds in the _Crop Field/Festival Campgrounds_, allowing both villagers and visitors to witness new technologies in action.  
  
Village-only demonstrations will have the flexibility to occur anywhere in the village that suits the project, with spaces available for various scales of experimentation and presentation.

### Crop Field/Festival Campgrounds

Deeper in the food forest …but not far from the main village… enclosed in a five foot tall hügelkultur wall, will be 75 acres dedicated to growing specific outdoor crops. With any luck, one of those will be industrial hemp (for soil-rejuvenating crop-rotation, fabrics, food, paper, natural cosmetic products, animal feed, bedding, biodegradable plastics, and building materials such as hempcrete).  
  
From time to time, this land will be used as festival camp-grounds for various, small arts and engineering festivals (likely seasonal events). Yurts will be provided as shelter for individuals and groups. Bathrooms (showers, sinks, incinerating toilets), which will be located in permanent domes, will be assigned to specific guests as well. Fresh food, if ready for harvesting, will be available on the hügelkultur wall and in the food forest (and accompanied with how-to guides). Additionally, the entry price to the festival will include free breakfast, lunch, and dinner at the Aquaponics Café.  
  
A feature of our festivals that some may find unusual is how we plan to handle after-festival clean-up. Not all festival go-ers may find this option attractive, but those that decide to stay behind and clean the areas they utilized during the festival will not only be returned their entry fee, but also paid 900 Circuitree, the village currency (this is quite close to what one of the residents would be paid to do the same job). If a festival go-er does decide to stay behind to help clean up, the minimum they will be required to clean in order to receive payment is the yurt and the bathroom that was assigned to them or their group (the amount will be the same regardless of whether their group helps them or not, as others will assist them instead).  
  
Note: This area may be one of the last established, well after the village is fully operational.

# Local terminology & long-term goals

## Frequencies

The development of a demonym for the people of a new society is inevitable, so we’ve chosen our own. The people of Gestalt Grove are officially known as _Frequencies_.  
  
In time, all inconsistent instances of "villagers", "artists", "residents", and "engineers" will be replaced with "Frequency/Frequencies"… except in the cases where a distinction is necessary between full-time residents, visiting guests staying for a predetermined amount of time, and those that are only there during certain hours to utilize studio resources.

## "fully operational"

Throughout the description of this village, you will often find the phrase "once the village is fully operational"… this is because there are a number of things that we want to do with the village that won’t actually be practical (or even possible) until the village is "fully operational".  
  
"Fully operational" means that the following conditions are met:

### Structures

every planned structure has been built

### Inhabited

at least 75% of the residential bedrooms are inhabited

### Greenhouses

the greenhouses have been producing food without serious malfunction for at least 24 weeks

### Water

plumbing (greywater plumbing, rainwater collection/filtration, and dew collection) has been without serious malfunction for at least 24 weeks

### Electricity

electricity has been without serious malfunction for at least 24 weeks (not counting severe-weather-induced outages)

### Operational Systems

the operational system (the private intranet that facilitates voting, ordering, inventory, etc.) has been without serious malfunction for at least 24 weeks

_(a serious malfunction is a malfunction that takes more than a day to repair)_

# Gestalt Grove’s Experiential School

At Gestalt Grove’s Experiential School, we believe education should be as unique and dynamic as the students themselves. Our Experiential School is designed to nurture curiosity, foster innovation, and empower students to explore their passions. Through a blend of self-directed learning, hands-on experiences, and a strong mentorship culture, we’re creating a model for education that goes beyond traditional classrooms.

### The Educational Approach: Interest-Driven Learning

Our students begin their journey with the "Intro to Everything" class, a modular learning experience designed to introduce them to the many fields of knowledge available. This class is organized into grouped segments, allowing students to explore broad categories like arts, sciences, technology, and humanities. Students can dive deeper into any subject that catches their interest, moving seamlessly between modules as their passions evolve. They can always return to the "Intro to Everything" class whenever they’re ready to explore new areas.  
  
From here, students are free to follow their interests, selecting relevant courses that align with their personal goals. Learning at Gestalt Grove is not restricted by predefined paths or time frames, it is driven entirely by the student’s curiosity and ambitions. This gives them the opportunity to explore, innovate, fail, and succeed in an environment that values the journey as much as the destination.

### Balancing Autonomy and Core Skills

While students have the freedom to choose their path, we ensure they are well-equipped with essential life skills. All students take classes in reading, writing, compassion, basic survival, gardening, permaculture practices, sustainable living, and emotional intelligence, among others. These foundational subjects are taught through hands-on, problem-based learning, making even the most basic skills engaging and relevant.  
  
To further enhance their personal development, students are encouraged to learn both a foreign language and a fictional language, broadening their cognitive abilities and creative thinking. Technology, including AI-powered resources, helps bridge gaps in subject matter expertise and makes language learning accessible to all.

### Mentorships and Apprenticeships: Learning from Experience

Our educational model is grounded in human connection. We emphasize mentorships and apprenticeships, pairing students with mentors who guide them through their learning journey. Every student benefits from personalized guidance, ensuring they receive support tailored to their unique strengths and challenges.  
  
Our mentor-student matching system ensures that students connect with mentors who align with their interests and learning styles, fostering strong, meaningful relationships that enrich both the student and mentor.

### A Holistic Approach to Learning

At Gestalt Grove, education goes beyond academics. Students participate in courses that focus on social intelligence, self-care, food preservation, and environmental stewardship, ensuring they are prepared not just for careers, but for life. We teach them to navigate uncertainty, problem-solve creatively, and apply their knowledge to real-world situations.  
  
Our curriculum encourages students to become independent thinkers while recognizing the value of collaboration and community. We believe that by nurturing these values early, students will grow into adults capable of navigating the complexities of the modern world with empathy, resilience, and ingenuity.

### Project-Based Learning and Community Engagement

Students are encouraged to apply what they learn through project-based learning. Every project, whether in the arts, sciences, or another field, is an opportunity for students to explore their ideas, take risks, and showcase their achievements. Regular project fairs are held to celebrate these creations, giving students the chance to share their work with the wider community.  
  
These fairs are not just about recognition; they are about learning from one another, fostering collaboration, and building confidence. Students receive certificates of achievement, sometimes accompanied by Circuitree rewards, to honor their growth and contributions.

### Technology as a Tool, Not a Replacement

While we utilize cutting-edge technology, including AI and customized learning algorithms, we maintain a focus on human interaction. Technology serves as a valuable tool to enhance learning but does not replace the role of general instructors or mentors. Our school ensures students receive the best of both worlds: the efficiency of AI-driven instruction paired with the emotional intelligence of human educators.  
  
Our general instructors—who are versatile, curious, and passionate—serve as guides for students across a broad range of subjects. These instructors benefit from regular retreats, workshops, and professional development sessions that foster a collaborative learning environment and prevent burnout.

### Financial Sustainability and Accessibility

We are committed to making our education accessible. Our school operates on a sliding scale tuition, ensuring that all families, regardless of financial situation, can benefit from our programs. The school is supported by a percentage of the village’s profits, and students have access to village resources, including food, facilities, and the ever-growing library.  
  
In addition to sliding-scale tuition, we’re actively seeking grants and external funding to ensure the long-term sustainability of the school. This approach allows us to continue providing high-quality education without compromising the rich experiences and resources we offer.  
  
Some students are able to attend for free. Each year, we offer a limited number of spots: 3 for early-year students and 3 for students of any age. These spots are awarded based on criteria like auditions, financial need, the strength of a student’s learning proposal, or their potential to contribute to their community.

### The Future of Education

At Gestalt Grove’s Experiential School, we believe education should be as fluid and adaptive as the world around us. By prioritizing interests, honoring individuality, and fostering deep connections, we’re helping students not only discover their passions but also equipping them with the tools they need to make meaningful contributions to the world.  
  
Our school is more than a place of learning, it’s a sanctuary where creativity, engineering, and innovation thrive without the pressures of conventional education. Join us in rethinking what education can be, and help us create a model for future generations.
